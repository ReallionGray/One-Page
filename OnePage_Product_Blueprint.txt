ONE PAGE — PRODUCT BLUEPRINT
Version 1.0 | 10 August 2026
Status: Foundational product and delivery blueprint

This document is the working source of truth for product direction, business requirements,
technical requirements, delivery sequencing, governance, and launch readiness. It is deliberately
country-neutral in its platform design, but payroll must not be implemented until the first launch
country and its legal/compliance model are formally selected.

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

One Page is a modular, multi-tenant operating platform for growing organizations. It connects
people, payroll, purchasing, expenses, assets, approvals, documents, reporting, and—later—inventory,
point of sale, finance, CRM, and industry workflows in one coherent system.

The product thesis is:

  Growing companies should be able to run their recurring people-and-money processes in one
  trusted place, with clear ownership, configurable approvals, and a complete audit trail.

One Page is not initially a full ERP. Its advantage is the shared control plane:

  one organization model, one identity layer, one workflow system, one entitlement system,
  one audit trail, and consistent cross-module user experience.

Recommended launch wedge:

  Core platform + HR + one-country Payroll + Procurement + basic Asset Management,
  supported by reporting, documents, import/export, audit, and accounting integrations.

Point of Sale, advanced Inventory, full Accounting, CRM, manufacturing, and industry-specific
modules are expansion tracks, not launch commitments.

2. VISION, MISSION, AND POSITIONING

Vision
  Every growing business has a simple, trusted operating system for its core processes.

Mission
  Make business operations visible, controlled, and easy to execute for organizations that have
  outgrown spreadsheets, disconnected applications, and informal approvals.

Positioning
  One Page is the configurable operating layer for growing businesses—connecting people,
  approvals, money, assets, and daily operations in one system.

Core promise
  One Page gives leaders visibility, gives teams simple self-service, and gives finance, HR,
  and operations the controls needed to execute confidently.

3. TARGET MARKET AND CUSTOMER SEGMENTS

Initial ideal customer profile

  * 20–500 employees, with an initial preference for 50–500.
  * Multiple departments, branches, locations, or legal entities.
  * A recurring need for payroll, leave, approvals, purchases, expenses, or asset tracking.
  * Current reliance on spreadsheets, email, WhatsApp, paper, or several disconnected systems.
  * A responsible HR, finance, operations, or accounting owner.
  * Willingness to adopt a subscription product and participate in assisted onboarding.

Best initial verticals

  * Retail and distribution
  * Professional services
  * Schools and education groups
  * Healthcare groups and pharmacies
  * Hospitality and restaurants
  * NGOs and development organizations
  * Logistics and field-service businesses

The first launch must choose one primary segment, one buyer, and one country. Vertical-specific
templates should follow customer evidence rather than being built speculatively.

Personas

  Founder/CEO: wants visibility, control, and exception-based reporting.
  Finance lead: wants accurate payroll, spend controls, reconciliations, and audit evidence.
  HR manager: owns employee records, leave, onboarding, documents, and employee experience.
  Operations manager: coordinates branches, assets, tasks, and approvals.
  Department manager: approves requests quickly within delegated authority.
  Employee: accesses payslips, submits leave and expenses, and tracks requests from a phone.
  Buyer/procurement officer: manages vendors, purchase requests, orders, and receipts.
  Asset custodian: knows where assets are, who has them, and what condition they are in.
  External accountant: needs reliable exports, traceability, and controlled access.
  System administrator: manages users, roles, entitlements, policies, and integrations.
  Branch/store manager: later manages attendance, inventory, POS, cash, and local approvals.

4. JOBS TO BE DONE

Executive
  When I need to know whether the company is operating properly, show me important exceptions
  without asking several people for spreadsheets.

Finance
  When money is requested, purchased, reimbursed, or paid, give me a controlled process with
  evidence, approvals, and reconciliation.

HR
  When an employee joins, changes role, takes leave, or exits, keep the record accurate and
  update dependent processes safely.

Employee
  When I need a document or want to submit a request, let me do it quickly without chasing HR
  or finance.

Operations
  When branches operate differently, enforce common policies while allowing controlled local
  configuration.

Accountant
  When I close a period, give me complete, traceable data that can be exported or reconciled.

5. PRODUCT SCOPE AND MODULE MAP

Shared platform foundation — launch-critical

  * Tenant, legal-entity, branch, department, location, and cost-center management
  * Users, memberships, teams, roles, permissions, and scoped access
  * Subscription plans, add-ons, entitlements, usage, and billing states
  * Approval workflows, tasks, delegation, escalation, and notifications
  * Documents, attachments, search, comments, and activity timelines
  * Audit log, reporting, dashboards, exports, and data import
  * API, webhooks, integrations, background jobs, and observability
  * Feature flags, configuration, data retention, export, and deletion controls

Human Resources — launch

Owns employee lifecycle and people records:

  * Employee master record and employment history
  * Departments, positions, managers, branches, and cost centers
  * Onboarding/offboarding checklists
  * Leave policies, balances, requests, approvals, and holidays
  * Employee documents and expiry alerts
  * Basic attendance import
  * Employee self-service

Deferred HR: recruitment marketplace, advanced performance management, learning, succession,
and complex workforce planning.

Payroll — launch, one jurisdiction

Owns payroll calculations and payroll-period records, consuming HR data:

  * Pay groups, calendars, pay frequencies, and payroll periods
  * Earnings, allowances, deductions, benefits, taxes, and statutory rules
  * Input validation, preview, variance analysis, approval, lock, and publish
  * Payslips, statutory reports, bank/payment export, and accounting export
  * Corrections, reversals, off-cycle processing, and audit history

Payroll must use effective-dated, versioned country rules. Closed periods must be reproducible and
must not change when a current employee record changes. Compliance claims require qualified local
tax/payroll review.

Procurement and Expenses — launch

  * Vendor master and supporting documents
  * Purchase requisitions and approval chains
  * Purchase orders and status tracking
  * Goods/service receipt
  * Budget threshold checks
  * Employee expense claims and reimbursement workflow
  * Basic spend reporting and accounting export

Deferred Procurement: competitive sourcing, tenders, contract lifecycle, full invoice automation,
three-way matching, corporate cards, and advanced spend analytics.

Asset Management — launch

  * Asset register, categories, serial numbers, and acquisition source
  * Assignment to an employee, department, branch, or location
  * Condition, custody, transfer, return, maintenance, and warranty dates
  * Disposal workflow and complete asset history
  * Depreciation inputs or controlled export to accounting

Inventory — expansion

  * Items, SKUs, units of measure, warehouses, stock movements, transfers, counts, adjustments,
    reorder thresholds, and valuation.

Point of Sale — expansion

  * Stores, registers, cash sessions, products, prices, sales, refunds, discounts, receipts,
    payments, cash close, and inventory movement.
  * Requires offline operation, device support, payment-provider assessment, reconciliation,
    fraud controls, and PCI responsibility boundaries.

Finance and Accounting — integration first, full module later

  * Launch: chart-of-account mapping, journal exports, payment references, reconciliation support.
  * Later: general ledger, AP, AR, bank reconciliation, budgets, tax, period close, and consolidation.

Additional expansion modules

  * CRM and customer management
  * Project and work management
  * Fleet and logistics
  * School administration
  * Clinic/pharmacy operations
  * NGO grants and project reporting
  * Field-service management
  * Recruitment and learning

6. MVP DEFINITION

MVP outcome
  A design-partner organization can manage its core employee lifecycle and controlled purchasing
  processes in One Page, complete a verified one-country payroll run, and produce evidence-backed
  reports and exports without engineering intervention.

MVP includes

  1. Core administration and organization structure
  2. Identity, roles, scoped permissions, and MFA-ready access
  3. Subscription/entitlement engine and billing adapter
  4. Audit, notifications, documents, import/export, and reporting
  5. HR core, leave, onboarding/offboarding, and employee self-service
  6. One-country Payroll for a deliberately limited compensation model
  7. Procurement requests, approvals, vendors, purchase orders, and receipts
  8. Basic expenses and reimbursements
  9. Basic Asset Management
  10. Accounting/bank export integrations as needed by design partners

MVP exclusions

  * Full POS and offline retail operation
  * Full accounting ledger
  * Multi-country payroll
  * Complex commission, equity, contractor, or cross-border payroll
  * Advanced inventory or manufacturing
  * Recruitment marketplace, LMS, advanced performance
  * Embedded lending, banking, or payment custody
  * Advanced AI automation

MVP product rule
  Every feature should clearly answer who is involved, what money/entitlement/asset is affected,
  and who approved it, when, and under which policy.

7. REPRESENTATIVE USER STORIES AND ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

Administration

  * As an administrator, I can create legal entities, branches, departments, locations, and
    cost centers so that records can be scoped correctly.
  * As an administrator, I can invite a user, assign roles and scopes, and remove access.
  * As an auditor, I can see who changed a record, what changed, when, from where, and why.
  * As an organization owner, I can export my data and understand what happens if a module is
    downgraded or cancelled.

Entitlements

  * As an account owner, I can subscribe to a plan and add or remove modules without redeploying.
  * As a system, I reject backend requests when the tenant lacks the required entitlement,
    even if the UI is bypassed.
  * As an account owner, when a module is disabled, historical records remain readable and
    exportable according to policy; new transactions are blocked or read-only.
  * As a billing operator, I can apply a grace period, retry failed payments, and audit every
    subscription change.

HR

  * As HR, I can create an employee with employment, tax, bank, emergency-contact, and document
    information.
  * As HR, I can onboard an employee using a checklist with owners, due dates, and evidence.
  * As an employee, I can view my profile and request a correction without editing protected fields.
  * As an employee, I can request leave and see balance, approvers, status, and decision history.
  * As HR, I can offboard an employee and trigger an access-review checklist.

Payroll

  * As a payroll officer, I can select a pay group, validate inputs, and preview expected results.
  * As a payroll officer, I cannot approve my own payroll run.
  * As finance, I can compare the current run with the previous run and investigate variances.
  * As an authorized approver, I can approve and lock a payroll period; later corrections require
    a controlled reversal or adjustment.
  * As an employee, I can access only my own payslips.
  * As an accountant, I can export a payroll journal and statutory reports with a run identifier.

Procurement and expenses

  * As a staff member, I can submit a purchase request with cost center, amount, justification,
    vendor information, and attachments.
  * As a manager, I can approve only requests within my scope and delegated authority.
  * As a buyer, I can turn an approved request into a purchase order without re-keying data.
  * As a receiver, I can record partial or full receipt and attach evidence.
  * As an employee, I can submit an expense claim and track approval and reimbursement status.
  * As finance, I can prevent payment when required approvals or evidence are missing.

Assets

  * As an asset custodian, I can register an asset and assign it to a person or location.
  * As a manager, I can approve transfers and see the custody history.
  * As finance, I can export acquisition and depreciation inputs.
  * As an administrator, I can dispose of an asset only through an auditable approval workflow.

8. BUSINESS REQUIREMENTS

BR-001 Product focus
  The first release shall target one country, one primary ICP, one buyer, and one measurable
  customer outcome.

BR-002 Multi-tenant operation
  A customer account shall support one or more legal entities, branches, departments, locations,
  cost centers, and users while preserving strict tenant isolation.

BR-003 Configurable control
  Customers shall configure approval thresholds, approvers, delegation, escalation, leave rules,
  payroll inputs, and module settings without custom code for normal use cases.

BR-004 Segregation of duties
  The system shall support maker-checker, approval limits, self-approval prevention, and scoped
  access for payroll, purchases, payments, refunds, sensitive HR fields, and asset disposal.

BR-005 Auditability
  Material actions and privileged access shall have an immutable or tamper-evident, tenant-scoped
  audit record, available to authorized users and exportable.

BR-006 Payroll controls
  Payroll shall support preview, variance analysis, versioned rules, effective dates, period lock,
  correction/reversal, approval evidence, reconciliation, and reproducible historical results.

BR-007 Migration
  The product shall import employee, department, location, leave, compensation, vendor, asset,
  user, and opening data through validated templates with exception and reconciliation reports.

BR-008 Subscription lifecycle
  The product shall support trial, active, grace period, suspended, expired, read-only, upgrade,
  downgrade, add-ons, usage limits, cancellation, export, and reactivation states.

BR-009 Data rights and exit
  Customers shall be able to export their data in documented formats. Retention, deletion, legal
  hold, correction, and access-request workflows shall be defined per jurisdiction and contract.

BR-010 Localization
  The system shall support time zones, currencies, locale formatting, regional addresses and
  phone numbers, effective-dated country rules, and country-specific document/report formats.

BR-011 Reporting
  Reports shall identify their source, scope, period, freshness, filters, and permissions. Payroll
  and financial reports must reconcile to authoritative transaction records.

BR-012 Customer operations
  The service shall provide onboarding, support tiers, incident communication, runbooks, SLOs,
  status visibility, and payroll-period escalation coverage.

9. SUBSCRIPTION AND ENTITLEMENT MODEL

Packaging recommendation

  Starter: core administration, HR records, leave, basic approvals, basic reporting.
  People: Starter + payroll, attendance, employee self-service, payslips, statutory reports.
  Operations: People + procurement, expenses, assets, budgets, branch/cost-center controls.
  Commerce: Operations + inventory, POS, store operations, cash reconciliation.
  Enterprise: multi-entity/country, SSO, advanced permissions, custom workflows, enhanced audit,
    dedicated support, SLA, data residency, and integration capacity.

Use a hybrid commercial model:

  * Base platform fee per organization
  * Active employees for HR/payroll
  * Named operators for finance/POS
  * Branches, legal entities, registers, or transaction volume where value is usage-based
  * Paid implementation, migration, premium support, and integrations

Entitlement dimensions

  * Module: module.hr, module.payroll, module.procurement, module.assets, module.pos
  * Feature: feature.hr.performance, feature.payroll.off_cycle, feature.procurement.matching
  * Limits: users, active_employees, branches, registers, storage_gb, API_calls, transactions
  * Country pack and regulatory scope
  * Retention period, reporting depth, support tier, and dedicated infrastructure

Plan versions must be immutable. Subscription changes reference a plan version and effective date.
The entitlement decision must return enabled/disabled, source, limit, current usage, effective date,
and grace-period status.

Entitlements and permissions are separate:

  Entitlement: the tenant subscribes to Payroll.
  Permission: this user may run payroll for Legal Entity A.
  Both checks must pass on the server.

Backend entitlement checks are required at endpoints, commands, jobs, event consumers, exports,
reports, and integrations. UI hiding is only a usability feature.

10. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE

Architecture principle
  Modules own their domain data and rules. The platform owns tenancy, identity, entitlements,
  workflow infrastructure, billing, notifications, documents, audit, and integration infrastructure.

Recommended starting shape
  Begin with a modular monolith, not distributed microservices:

  * One deployable backend with strict domain boundaries
  * One PostgreSQL cluster with separate schemas or namespaces per bounded context
  * Internal module interfaces and architecture tests
  * Transactional outbox and durable background jobs
  * Independent frontend module boundaries inside one application shell
  * Clear extraction criteria based on scale, compliance, team ownership, or availability

Logical architecture

  Web / mobile / partner API
          |
  CDN/WAF + API gateway/BFF
          |
  Identity | Tenant context | Permissions | Entitlements
          |
  Workflows | Audit | Billing | Files | Notifications | Search | Reporting
          |
  HR | Payroll | Procurement | Expenses | Assets | Inventory | POS | Finance
          |
  PostgreSQL | Redis | object storage | queue | analytics read models

Tenant hierarchy

  Platform
    Customer account / tenant
      Legal entities
        Business units / branches / locations / departments / warehouses
          Users, teams, employees, vendors, customers, and records

Tenant isolation tiers

  Shared: tenant_id on every tenant row, database defense-in-depth, automated isolation tests.
  Dedicated database: for larger or special-compliance customers.
  Dedicated environment: for regulated enterprise or data-residency requirements.

Never trust a tenant ID supplied only in a request body. Tenant context must be derived from the
authenticated session, selected organization, API client, or verified partner context.

Identity and authorization

  * OIDC authentication; SAML/SCIM for enterprise tiers.
  * MFA for administrators; WebAuthn or TOTP support.
  * Short-lived tokens and rotating refresh tokens.
  * A user may belong to multiple tenants with different roles.
  * Use RBAC plus scoped/attribute-based access: legal entity, department, branch, manager chain,
    employment status, sensitivity, time validity, amount limit, and delegation.
  * Support joiner/mover/leaver flows, service accounts, API keys, session controls, recovery,
    and time-limited support elevation with reason and full audit.

Canonical bounded contexts

  Platform/Tenancy: tenants, legal entities, locations, users, memberships, roles, subscriptions.
  Party/Organization: reusable employees, suppliers, customers, contacts, teams, addresses.
  HR: employee lifecycle, contracts, leave, attendance, documents, performance later.
  Payroll: pay groups, periods, calculations, payslips, statutory outputs, approvals.
  Procurement: requisitions, suppliers, POs, receipts, invoices/matching later.
  Expenses: claims, policies, approvals, reimbursements.
  Assets: register, assignment, maintenance, depreciation inputs, disposal.
  Inventory: items, SKUs, stock movements, locations, counts, valuation.
  POS: stores, registers, cash sessions, sales, refunds, payments, offline sync.
  Finance: chart of accounts, journals, AP/AR, reconciliation, period close later.
  Reporting: report definitions, read models, dashboards, exports, data access policy.

Data ownership rules

  Employee profile -> HR owns; Payroll and reporting consume.
  Payroll result -> Payroll owns; Finance and employee portal consume.
  Supplier -> Party/Procurement owns; purchasing and finance consume.
  Purchase order -> Procurement owns; Inventory and Finance consume.
  Stock movement -> Inventory owns; POS and reporting consume.
  Asset depreciation -> Assets owns; Finance and reporting consume.
  Sale -> POS owns; Inventory and Finance consume.
  Subscription -> Billing owns; Entitlement service consumes.

Do not allow modules to write another module's tables or directly post arbitrary accounting entries.
Use stable contracts and events.

Workflow requirements

Workflows are a platform capability, with versioned definitions and persisted instances. Support:

  * Triggers, states, tasks, conditions, approvers, amount thresholds
  * Delegation with effective dates
  * Escalation, reminders, SLAs, rejection/rework, cancellation, and expiry
  * Comments, attachments, decision evidence, and complete transition history
  * Self-approval and circular-approval prevention
  * Idempotent commands and durable retries
  * Existing instances continuing on their original workflow version

Use a persisted state machine first. Consider Temporal/Camunda only when workflow complexity and
operational evidence justify it.

Audit requirements

Audit authentication, permissions, subscriptions, HR changes, payroll calculations and approvals,
purchase approvals, inventory adjustments, asset disposal, refunds, exports, support access,
integration credentials, and workflow transitions.

Audit record should include: event ID, tenant, actor and delegated actor, action, resource, before/
after or relevant change, reason, source, IP/user agent where appropriate, correlation ID, and time.
Store append-only or tamper-evident records. Application logs are not a substitute.

API and event requirements

  * REST/OpenAPI with /api/v1 versioning initially.
  * Consistent errors, pagination, filtering, rate limits, ETags/version checks, correlation IDs.
  * Idempotency keys for commands that create or move money/periods/stock.
  * Public contracts must not expose internal database models.
  * Use transactional outbox for cross-module events.
  * Events are tenant-scoped, versioned, immutable, traceable, and idempotently consumed.
  * Suggested events: employee.hired, employee.terminated, payroll.period.approved,
    purchase_order.approved, goods_receipt.completed, asset.assigned, subscription.changed,
    pos.sale.completed, workflow.task.created.

Integration hub

Adapters may be added for accounting, banks/payment providers, tax/statutory services, email/SMS,
identity, attendance devices, e-commerce, shipping, POS hardware, CRM, and data warehouses.
Each adapter needs credential storage, signed webhook verification, retry/replay, rate limits,
mapping, dead-letter handling, synchronization history, reconciliation, and customer-visible health.

Billing

One Page billing should be provider-neutral. Provider adapters may include Stripe, Paystack,
Flutterwave, or enterprise invoicing, subject to market and regulatory review. The internal system
owns plans, plan versions, subscriptions, usage, invoices, credits, proration, grace periods,
cancellation, and entitlement activation. Provider webhooks must be signature-verified and idempotent.

Files

Use object storage for documents and store metadata in PostgreSQL. Require tenant isolation,
encryption, malware scanning, content-type/size validation, quarantine, versioning, retention,
access logging, short-lived signed URLs, and legal hold support.

Frontend

Use one application shell with tenant switcher, global search, notifications, user controls, and
module navigation. Use a shared design system for forms, tables, approvals, status, audit timelines,
empty/error states, and upgrade/read-only states. Backend enforcement is authoritative.

Reference technology baseline

  * Backend: ASP.NET Core or NestJS; select one after team and repository review.
  * Frontend: React + TypeScript.
  * Data: PostgreSQL, Redis, object storage, managed queue.
  * Deployment: containers on a managed platform; introduce Kubernetes only if justified.
  * Identity: managed OIDC/SAML provider.
  * Observability: OpenTelemetry-compatible logs, metrics, and traces.
  * Infrastructure: infrastructure-as-code and automated CI/CD.

11. SECURITY, PRIVACY, AND CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Baseline security

  * TLS everywhere, encryption at rest, KMS-managed keys, and secrets manager.
  * MFA, secure sessions, rate limiting, CSRF protection, input validation, secure headers.
  * Least-privilege cloud IAM and time-bound privileged support access.
  * SAST, DAST, dependency/container scanning, patching, penetration testing, and vulnerability
    management.
  * No passwords, tokens, full bank accounts, government IDs, or payroll secrets in logs.
  * Tenant identity in every request, job, event, cache key, file path, search query, and analytics
    pipeline.

Data governance

  * Data classification for public, internal, confidential, personal, and highly sensitive data.
  * Retention/deletion schedule per record type and jurisdiction.
  * Legal holds, subject access/correction/export workflow, breach response, and subprocessor register.
  * Data processing agreements and privacy impact assessments where appropriate.
  * Data residency and cross-border processing assessment before launch.
  * Named ownership for privacy, security, payroll compliance, and incident response.

Nigeria-first assessment (if selected)

The platform should be Africa-ready, but country-specific claims must be professionally validated.
For a Nigeria launch, assess applicable Nigerian data protection obligations and payroll/statutory
requirements, including PAYE, pension, and other relevant deductions and employer obligations. Isolate
country rules in an effective-dated country pack; do not hardcode national rules throughout the core.

Financial and payroll controls

  * Decimal money arithmetic, explicit currencies, rounding and exchange-rate policy.
  * Effective-dated tax/rule versions and reproducible calculation inputs.
  * Payroll cut-off, preview, variance analysis, maker-checker, lock, publish, and reconciliation.
  * Controlled bank export; before/after payment reconciliation.
  * Reversal and adjustment instead of destructive edits to closed records.
  * Segregation of vendor creation, PO approval, payment authorization, and bank-detail changes.
  * Period close controls and access recertification.

12. NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

Initial targets (to be validated during architecture and capacity planning):

  Availability: 99.9% monthly for core SaaS.
  Standard API reads: p95 under 500 ms under expected load.
  Event delivery: at-least-once with idempotent consumers.
  Tenant isolation: zero cross-tenant access in automated and independent security tests.
  Recovery point objective: target 15 minutes or better.
  Recovery time objective: target 1–4 hours initially.
  Accessibility: WCAG 2.1 AA target.
  Data export: documented, tenant-scoped, observable, and resumable for large tenants.
  Auditability: 100% of defined sensitive actions recorded.
  Payroll: 100% of runs reconciled before approval in pilot.

Capacity testing must cover payroll-period peaks, workflow runs, documents, imports, exports,
integrations, audit volume, concurrent administrators, large tenants, poor connectivity, and regional
latency—not just employee count.

13. DELIVERY ROADMAP AND PHASE GATES

Phase 0 — Product framing and investment decision (2–4 weeks)

Deliver: selected country, legal/compliance matrix, primary ICP and buyer, three design partners,
initial module bundle, business model, regulatory assessment, feasibility assessment, and go/no-go.

Gate: country and compliance owner selected; customer evidence supports the wedge; payroll and data
protection risks have named owners; scope is small enough to build.

Phase 1 — Discovery and service design (6–8 weeks)

Map happy and exception paths for hire-to-pay, leave, payroll correction, requisition-to-purchase,
receipt, asset assignment, offboarding, reporting, and subscription downgrade. Produce process maps,
prototypes, PRDs, acceptance criteria, canonical data model, permission model, country payroll spec,
threat model, control matrix, migration templates, and pilot hypotheses.

Gate: customer and specialist sign-off on processes, backlog, controls, data model, and prototypes.

Phase 2 — Platform foundation (8–12 weeks)

Build tenant model, IAM, scoped permissions, entitlements, billing adapter, audit, workflows,
notifications, documents, import framework, APIs, jobs, search, reporting foundation, observability,
backup/recovery, admin console, and support tooling.

Gate: safe tenant provisioning; automated cross-tenant tests; server-side permissions and entitlements;
auditable sensitive actions; tested backups; observable/retryable jobs; critical workflow tests.

Phase 3 — MVP modules (4–6 months)

Build HR, limited one-country Payroll, Procurement, Expenses, Assets, and reports. Payroll starts
with salaried employees, standard earnings/deductions, one or two frequencies, payslips, reports,
approval/lock, bank export, correction/reversal, and audit.

Gate: three design partners can complete target workflows; payroll calculation scenarios pass; data
imports reconcile; no unresolved critical tenant/security defects; support can diagnose normal issues.

Phase 4 — Controlled pilot (8–12 weeks)

Use 3–5 customers with executive sponsor, implementation lead, migration owner, payroll SME, weekly
review, rollback plan, and support escalation. Run payroll in parallel with the existing process for
2–3 cycles and compare gross, taxable pay, deductions, net, employer cost, variances, bank totals,
reports, and accounting reconciliation.

Gate: 80%+ target workflows complete without engineering intervention; every payroll variance is
explained; migration is signed off; no critical/high security issue remains; support targets hold.

Phase 5 — General availability (target month 12–15)

Require production support, SLOs, incident response, disaster recovery test, terms/privacy/DPA,
onboarding playbooks, pricing, tested billing/cancellation/downgrade/refund flows, status page,
release process, known-limitations documentation, and customer communications.

Phase 6 — Expansion (after evidence)

Add advanced expenses/procurement, accounting integrations, budgeting, mobile, performance,
recruitment, Inventory, and a second country only after stability, retention, supportability, and
gross-margin evidence. POS gets its own discovery/certification track for offline, hardware,
payments, cash, inventory, security, and PCI concerns.

14. TEAM AND OPERATING MODEL

MVP team (indicative 14–20 people)

  * Product/program: product lead, PM, BA/product operations, delivery lead, 1–2 designers.
  * Engineering: architect/tech lead, 4–6 backend, 2–3 frontend, platform/DevOps, data/integration.
  * Quality/control: 2 QA, automation, security specialist, payroll/financial-controls specialist.
  * Operations: implementation lead, customer support lead, enablement/documentation.

Payroll, finance controls, privacy, and security expertise must be embedded before implementation,
not added during final testing.

Governance forums

  Product Steering Committee: scope, funding, launch, market expansion, risk acceptance.
  Architecture Review Board: domain boundaries, data, security, reliability, build/buy decisions.
  Compliance and Controls Committee: payroll rules, statutory changes, access, audit, retention,
    reconciliation, and control failures.
  Release Readiness Review: product, engineering, QA, security, operations, and payroll-control signoff.

Each module must maintain: owner, API contract, data ownership statement, permissions, entitlements,
workflow definitions, event catalog, audit requirements, retention, dashboards, runbook, threat model,
and test strategy.

Customer onboarding

  1. Contract/subscription setup
  2. Tenant configuration
  3. Role and approval design
  4. Trial migration and reconciliation
  5. User training
  6. Payroll parallel run
  7. Go-live readiness review
  8. Hypercare
  9. Success review

Support tiers

  Tier 1: guidance, configuration, imports, basic troubleshooting.
  Tier 2: workflow, integration, data, and permission issues.
  Tier 3: payroll defects, security incidents, corruption, outages, engineering defects.

Payroll and security incidents require expedited escalation and protected payroll-period coverage.

15. DATA MIGRATION STRATEGY

Stages

  1. Inventory source systems and data owners.
  2. Map to canonical One Page model.
  3. Build transformations and templates.
  4. Run trial migration.
  5. Validate counts, duplicates, exceptions, and totals.
  6. Obtain customer sign-off.
  7. Run final migration and freeze/reconcile source changes.
  8. Perform post-migration verification and retain evidence.

Payroll reconciliation must cover employee count, gross, deductions, employer costs, net, year-to-date
values, statutory balances, and bank totals. Spreadsheet imports must be validated and approved before
they can affect payroll; no unvalidated upload may execute a payroll run.

16. QA, RELEASE, AND DISASTER RECOVERY

Test layers

  * Unit: payroll rules, money/rounding, entitlements, permissions, dates/time zones.
  * Integration: billing, identity, files, notifications, banking, accounting, imports/exports.
  * Contract: public APIs, internal module interfaces, webhooks, country packs.
  * End-to-end: hire-to-pay, leave, requisition-to-PO, PO-to-asset, payroll-to-export,
    subscription-to-entitlement, termination-to-access removal.
  * Non-functional: isolation, load, security, accessibility, recovery, failure/retry, offline later.

Release controls

  * Peer review, automated CI, migration review, feature flags, staged rollout, rollback plan,
    release notes, monitoring, and post-release review.
  * Payroll rules use effective dates and controlled releases; historical calculations never change.

Recovery

  * Point-in-time backups, encrypted backup copies, documented RPO/RTO, restoration tests at least
    quarterly, disaster runbooks, and customer communication procedures.
  * A failed payroll or migration requires a correction/rollback plan, not ordinary feature rollback.

North-star metric
  Percentage of a customer's recurring people-and-money processes completed in One Page with a
  complete audit trail.

Activation

  * Organization configured
  * First employee imported
  * First workflow created
  * First leave/expense/purchase request completed
  * First payroll run completed in parallel
  * First report exported

Product and quality

  * Employee self-service adoption
  * Two-or-more-module adoption
  * Workflow completion rate and approval time
  * Payroll processing time and reconciliation variance
  * Migration exception rate
  * Sensitive-action audit completeness
  * Failed job, API error, uptime, and restore success rate

Commercial

  * Trial-to-paid conversion
  * MRR/ARR, average revenue per organization
  * Gross retention, net retention, churn, expansion revenue
  * Customer acquisition cost and payback
  * Implementation time and support tickets per customer

Suggested pilot targets

  * 99.9% core availability
  * Zero unresolved critical security defects
  * 100% payroll runs reconciled before approval
  * 100% defined sensitive actions auditable
  * Less than 1% post-signoff migration correction rate
  * 90%+ core workflow completion without support intervention

18. PRINCIPAL RISKS AND MITIGATIONS

Overbuilding an ERP
  Mitigation: one ICP, one country, one wedge; expansion requires evidence.

Payroll error or regulatory non-compliance
  Mitigation: local specialist ownership, versioned rules, parallel runs, reconciliation, lock,
  maker-checker, controlled corrections, and effective dates.

Tenant data leak
  Mitigation: explicit isolation strategy, tenant-aware access everywhere, automated isolation tests,
  encryption, support-access controls, backup isolation, and independent security testing.

Weak authorization
  Mitigation: scoped/attribute-based permissions, field sensitivity, segregation of duties, access
  reviews, self-approval prevention, and time-bound delegation.

Migration failure
  Mitigation: templates, trial imports, exception/reconciliation reports, customer sign-off, rollback.

Subscription changes break historical access
  Mitigation: immutable plan versions, read-only historical access, export, grace periods, and audit.

Generic shared services create coupling
  Mitigation: module ownership, bounded contracts, schema boundaries, event conventions, architecture
  tests, and explicit extraction criteria.

Integration failure
  Mitigation: adapters, retries, replay, dead letters, mapping validation, health visibility,
  reconciliation, and manual fallback.

POS complexity
  Mitigation: defer; give POS a separate discovery, certification, offline, device, and payment track.

Customization becomes unprofitable services
  Mitigation: configurable policies and workflows with strict limits on bespoke code.

20. FINAL PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

One Page should scale by adding well-bounded modules to a trusted control platform. The product will
win less by launching the most modules and more by being accurate, secure, auditable, easy to adopt,
and dependable during the customer’s most important recurring processes.

Recommended sequence:

  1. Select country, ICP, buyer, and design partners.
  2. Build tenancy, identity, entitlements, workflows, audit, and migration foundations.
  3. Win trust through HR, one-country Payroll, and controlled approvals.
  4. Add expenses, procurement depth, assets, accounting integrations, and Inventory.
  5. Add POS and vertical modules only after evidence supports the investment.
  6. Preserve clean ownership, effective-dated rules, customer exit rights, and reliable controls.
